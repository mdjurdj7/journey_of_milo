# Region 1 — Detail v1

**Companion to:** Region Progression v1
**Status:** Working. Several threads open and flagged.

---

## 1. What the region is about

**Crossing into range.**

Not "a beach." The shoreline is where the player starts, but the region's subject is the walk inland — away from the sea, toward the tower, across the boundary where the signal begins to have effect.

This reframes the opening from *a starting point* to *a threshold*. The player came from somewhere that still had things in it. Everything from here inward has less.

### Water is a boundary, not a subject

Settled. The sea is the edge of the map and the thing at the player's back. It is not what the region is about, and it should not accumulate subject matter — no tidal geometry, no maritime detail, no wreckage, nothing the water did.

**Practical consequence:** the sea should leave the frame early. After the opening screens it becomes atmosphere behind the player rather than a feature beside them. This is the single largest constraint on existing background plates: any plate whose subject is water is the wrong subject.

---

## 2. Spatial logic

Arrival at the water's edge → inland movement → the land opens or rises → the tower becomes visible → continued movement inland.

**Direction of travel is away from the sea.** This matters for two reasons. It makes the sea a thing the character could turn back toward and doesn't, and it lets the tower's first appearance be an event rather than a given — the player should not see it on the first screen.

**Frame:** fully horizontal throughout. Nothing overhead. No verticals except the tower at extreme distance. Region 2 begins the compression; Region 1 spends none of it.

---

## 3. Condition — the least depleted region

Region 1's job in the gradient is to be the **benchmark**. Everything after it reads as emptier by comparison, which only works if there is something here to lose.

What that means concretely:

- Vegetation healthy and unremarkable. Nothing sick, nothing dead.
- Ground undisturbed. No damage anywhere in the region.
- Near-zero built structures. A coast doesn't need buildings, and holding them back here makes their arrival later meaningful.
- Roads, if any, faint. This is the outer edge; the deep worn paths belong further in.
- **Something is still here.** See §4.

---

## 4. The traveller — first appearance

One NPC, travelling the same direction as the player. He recurs across the run; see Region Progression §3.

### Function

They are the premise made visible before the player has any way to understand it. Someone is going to the tower. They believe what the character believes. Nothing about them explains why.

They are also the only thing in the region that acknowledges the player at all — which makes the rest of the world's silence legible by contrast.

### Constraints

**They do not explain.** No exposition about the tower, the signal, the exodus, or the world. They are going, and they assume anyone else out here already knows why. Any line that teaches the player the premise is the wrong line.

**They do not need anything.** No quest, no request, no recruitment, no escort. The player can approach or not.

**They do not stop.** Presence without transaction. They were going before the player arrived and they continue after.

### Recurrence — settled

**He recurs, with an end state.** Region 1 is his first appearance, not his only one. He is encountered repeatedly across the run, always ahead of the player, and he is at the tower at the end.

Full treatment is in Region Progression §3 (the traveller) and §5 (the tower encounter). What Region 1 owes that structure:

- **He must be established as unremarkable here.** First meeting sets the baseline against which every later meeting reads as change. Nothing dramatic, nothing memorable, no hook.
- **He must be in good condition here.** The walk has not cost him anything yet. Everything later is measured against this.
- **He must be ahead of the player when he leaves the frame.** The direction of the relationship is set in the first encounter and never inverts until the possible late-run pass.

---

## 5. Light

**Bright and empty, not cold and empty.** Settled.

High pale sky. Washed out rather than darkened. Light reflecting up off wet ground near the shore. Colorless brightness — a white sky rather than a blue one.

**No warm accent anywhere in the region.** Bible §15 explicitly permits scenes with none, and this is that scene. No golden hour, no orange, no fire.

The reasoning: loneliness in daylight does not explain itself, and gloom does. It also preserves the descent — if Region 1 is already dark, later regions have nowhere to go, and the tower silhouette reads far better against a pale sky than a grey one.

---

## 6. Room and event grammar

### Governing principle

**Nothing in this region was arranged for the player.**

No room is guarded, hidden, prepared, or set up as a challenge. Everything is found in the state it was left in, centuries ago, by people who believed they were going somewhere better. This is the inverse of standard roguelike room grammar, where a treasure room implies someone placed treasure and defended it.

The unifying image across every room type: **things set down and not picked up.** Mid-use, unhurried, no panic. That grammar covers treasure, events, and background dressing, and it states the premise without a word.

### Treasure

There is no treasure. There are **belongings.**

Someone set it down, or packed it and didn't take it. The player goes through it. The register is not discovery, it is **intrusion** — and the fact that the owner is not dead, merely gone, makes it worse rather than better.

### Rest

Direct collision with the genre default: rest means a campfire, and Region 1 permits no warm accent.

Treat this as an opportunity rather than a problem. **Rest without fire, in flat pale daylight,** is unusual and immediately signals a different game.

**Open:** what replaces the fire as the reason to stop — shelter, water, sitting down, or simply the act of stopping.

### Events

Events are **findings, not incidents.**

The premise holds that nothing happens here anymore. An event framed as a thing occurring *to* the player fights that; an event framed as the player discovering a state of affairs does not. A place where something was left mid-action. A fork where one path is worn and one is not.

### Shop

**Exchange without a merchant.** Settled in principle: no humanoid trader in this region.

Options considered:
- *Automated provisioning* — infrastructure still operating with no one supervising, dispensing to an empty road for centuries. Cheapest to justify, coldest, closest to the Blame! register.
- *Accumulation without an accumulator* — things pile up at a low point or a bend in the road, and the player takes from the pile and leaves something. Currency as custom with no institution behind it.
- *A creature that collects* — chosen. See below.
- *Someone who refused* — a person still trading out of habit. Warmest and most memorable, but they become the most interesting thing in the region and compete with the traveller. **Reserved for a later region,** where a single person who refused to go will land harder than it does in the opening.

### The collector

**It collects because it was bred or trained to, and it is still doing the job.**

Something that worked alongside people — fetched, carried, sorted, retrieved and handed over — and has continued for centuries with no one to hand things to. Same failure mode as the tower, at the scale of one animal: a function executing correctly with the owner gone. The shop becomes a small rhyme with the game's central premise, stated by nobody.

It also solves the mechanical problem the other options don't. A wild animal has no reason to give the player anything. An animal shaped to receive and hand over already does. Transaction without commerce, and without a merchant.

**Risk:** this is one decision away from the dog waiting at the station — loyal creature, absent master, cheap sentiment. That would undercut a world built on indifference.

**Guardrails:**

- **It is not waiting for the player and does not like them.** No recognition, no affection, no relief at seeing a person. The player is not an owner returning; they are the next thing in the queue. It processes them.
- **It is good at its job.** Practiced, quick, unbothered. A pitiable decrepit thing is sentimental; a competent one still performing a service for a species that left is colder and stranger.
- **No mammal warmth.** It should not invite petting. This is the difference between eerie and cute, and it is largely a silhouette decision.

**Placement:** it collected *for* people, so it should be where people were — but Region 1 holds built structures near zero. The resolution: it still comes to the spot where something used to stand, and there is nothing there. It is keeping an appointment with a building.

**Open — recurrence of the species.** Under the depletion principle (§7), the same creature further along the gradient — alone longer, in an emptier place — would have drifted further from the job. The last one encountered might still be collecting but no longer trading.

### Combat

The only room type requiring no re-registering. See §7.

## 7. Encounter logic

The enemies here are things that stayed — the ones that could not hear the signal, or refused it.

**Calibration principle:** strangeness scales with depletion. At the edge of range, whatever stayed has been alone for the least time and in the least emptied place. Region 1's encounters should be the most legible and least altered in the game. Things get stranger inward, not because difficulty rises, but because isolation has had longer to work.

This ties enemy design to the region gradient rather than to a difficulty curve, and it means Region 1's roster should read as *understandable* — which is a useful constraint on a first-region roster regardless.

---

## 8. What Region 1 must not do

- No ruins or rubble
- No damage of any kind
- No warm accent
- No gloom, no heavy weather, no darkness
- No verticals except the distant tower
- No explanation, from the traveller or from anything else
- No maritime subject matter beyond the opening screens
- No humanoid merchant
- No room that reads as having been arranged, guarded, or prepared for the player

## 9. Open threads

**Threshold marker.** Whether the boundary into range is physically marked — some edge the player crosses — or purely implied by the inland walk. Cheap to establish, significant implication. Unresolved.

**Arrival method.** Whether the character arrives by sea or is already ashore when the run begins. Affects whether the sea reads as origin or as scenery.

**What replaces the fire at rest nodes.** See §6.

**Whether the collector species recurs deeper in.** See §6.

**Non-traveller life.** Whether anything else is still present in Region 1 beyond the traveller and the collector — and if so, whether it is going, staying, or unaware. The benchmark works better with more than one data point.

**Tower's first appearance.** Where in the region it becomes visible, and whether that placement is fixed or procedural. Given the run graph is generated, a guaranteed sightline may need explicit handling.

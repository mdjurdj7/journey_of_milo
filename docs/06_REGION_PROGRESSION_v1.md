# Region Progression v1

**Status:** Working design context. Premise is settled in outline; region count and several structural questions are open and flagged below.

**Terminology:** *Region*, not biome. Consistent with Art Direction Bible §17 ("Regional Expression").

---

## 1. Premise

A tower was built as an evacuation mechanism, in response to the belief that the world was ending.

Everyone who could leave left through it, a very long time ago.

**The belief was wrong.** The world did not end. It is intact, healthy, bright, and quiet.

**The signal still runs.** The tower has been calling continuously since before anyone currently alive was born.

**The mechanism no longer works.** The call is transmitted perfectly. Nothing is delivered on the other end.

**The character does not know this.** They are walking toward the tower under the same assumption everyone else made.

### The critical qualifier

The signal is **not addressed to anyone**. It is not choosing, selecting, or summoning the character. It is a beacon still lit for an evacuation that ended, and the character is simply within range. Nothing in this world has a relationship with the player.

This is the load-bearing distinction. If the tower wants the character specifically, the premise collapses into a chosen-one story and loses the indifference that makes it work.

---

## 2. World state consequences

These follow directly from the premise and should constrain every asset and encounter.

**No catastrophe occurred.** No bodies, no signs of struggle, no evidence of force. People left in an orderly fashion during ordinary activity. Doors open. Things set down mid-use.

**Damage is forbidden; age is required.** These are different and the distinction is load-bearing. Nothing burned, was breached, impacted, or thrown down — anything implying force contradicts the premise. But the exodus was centuries ago, and everything people built has stood without maintenance since. Decay is therefore mandatory: roofs down from rot rather than collapse, walls opened by root growth and frost, floors gone, stairs going up to nothing. A world with no decay at all would read as *tended*, which is a different premise. Nothing should suggest a bad day; everything should suggest a very long absence of anyone caring.

**One thing is not decaying.** Everything people made is coming apart. The tower is intact and still running perfectly. That contrast is the single strongest image available to this premise, and it only exists if there is decay to contrast against.

**The land is fine.** Vegetation is healthy. Water is clean. Weather is normal. What is missing is anything that could hear a signal and walk toward it.

**Beauty is evidence.** Every bright, intact vista is an argument that leaving was unnecessary. The art direction is not mood — it is the game's central claim, made without dialogue.

**Emptiness is a gradient.** Depletion is strongest nearest the tower and weakest at the edge of range. The player can read their own proximity by how much is left around them.

**Paths have direction.** Roads running toward the tower are worn deep. Roads going anywhere else are faint.

**Built density is a second reading of the same gradient.** Settlement clusters along the roads that lead toward the tower and thins along the ones that don't. Same information as the worn paths, at a scale the player reads without being told. It also gives the emptiness a number: an empty coast is quiet, an empty town is *this many people, gone*.

**It is still happening.** Things still occasionally cross the frame, unhurried, heading the same way as the player, uninterested in them. Mostly things born long after the exodus, that never knew what the signal meant.

**Enemies are what stayed.** Three categories exist in this world: the ones still going, the ones that cannot hear it, and the ones that refused. The last two have been alone in a world they were told was over for a very long time. That is the enemy population, and it requires no separate justification.

---

## 3. Organizing principle

The run is ordered by **depletion**, not geography.

The player starts at the outer edge of the signal's range — the region with the most left in it — and moves inward toward the center, where nothing that could leave remains. Regions are therefore not a tour of terrain types; each one is a measurement of distance from the tower.

Three arcs run alongside this:

- **Light:** high-key and colorless at the start, progressively losing sky, ending in the only interior in the run.
- **Frame:** horizontal and open at the start, progressively compressing, ending vertical.
- **Built structures:** near-absent at the start, arriving with the depletion gradient. Ruins should appear *because* the player is getting closer, not as ambient scenery. Bible §18 names constant ruins as an anti-goal, and ruins are the most exhausted vocabulary in fantasy environment art — the protection is to make their arrival mean something. Regions 1 and 2 can carry almost none.

The tower is visible from every region and resolves with proximity: unreadable silhouette → readable structure → overhead mass → interior underfoot. This is the connective tissue across the entire run and is the cheapest structural device available — one shape carrying the run's sense of direction, with atmospheric perspective (Bible §4) doing the work.

### The traveller

A second connective device runs the length of the game: one figure, travelling the same direction as the player, encountered repeatedly.

**He is ahead of the player throughout.** Settled. The reasons:

- His condition is legible before the player's. He is further into the depletion gradient at every meeting, so what the walk costs shows on him first. The player reads their own future each time.
- It fits linear traversal. Someone ahead is caught up to — traces first, then him. Someone behind must overtake offscreen repeatedly, and every occurrence invites the question of how.
- It makes the summit his arrival. He reaches the door first and has been standing at it longer, which strengthens both branches of the final encounter.

The rejected alternative was placing him behind and gaining. That generates pursuit pressure, which is the wrong emotion for this premise — nothing is chasing the player, nothing else wants the door, and there is no clock. Urgency the fiction cannot support would read as an imported mechanic.

**He changes across encounters by condition, not dialogue.** Each meeting he is further along and more worn by the walk than the player is. This is what makes the final encounter land; without visible change across the run, his turn at the tower is unmotivated.

**Open:** severe deterioration and staying ahead eventually conflict. Either keep the change subtle, or let the last meeting before the tower be the one where the player passes him — in which case he arrives at the summit afterward rather than before. The second may be the stronger version.

His constraints as a character are in Region 1 Detail §4. His role at the summit is in §5 below.

## 4. Regions

Names below are functional placeholders. Naming is not settled here.

### Region 1 — Arrival / edge of range

**Function:** Establish the world as intact rather than ruined. Establish scale and solitude. Establish the tower.

**Condition:** The least depleted region in the run. Things are still here. This should be visibly true in comparison to everything that follows.

**Light:** Bright and empty, not cold and empty. High pale sky, salt glare, light reflecting up off wet ground. Washed out rather than darkened. No warm accent anywhere in the region (Bible §15).

**Frame:** Fully horizontal. Wide, open, nothing overhead.

**Tower:** Barely resolvable. A pale silhouette merging with atmospheric illumination at extreme distance (Bible §5).

**Note:** This region carries the premise's first argument — loneliness in daylight, which does not explain itself, rather than loneliness in gloom, which does.

---

### Region 2 — Living and silent

**Function:** First evidence that something is wrong. A beach being empty is normal; this is not.

**Condition:** Vegetation thriving. Dense, healthy, sunlit. No animal life of any kind — no birdsong, no insects, nothing in the undergrowth. The absence is specific, not general.

**Light:** Retains the high-key sky from Region 1. Do not spend the darkness here.

**Frame:** First vertical elements after a purely horizontal opening. Compression begins without going underground.

**Tower:** Readable as a structure.

**Open:** A drained basin — cracked pale ground, salt crust, old shorelines visible above head height — was considered as an alternative to woodland. More distinctive, harder to compose from (mostly ground plane and sky, few silhouettes). Unresolved.

---

### Region 3 — Deep range

**Function:** The register turns. This is where the depletion gradient becomes undeniable and where whatever stayed has been alone longest.

**Condition:** Heavily emptied. Roads here are worn to trenches. Anything still present is here because it refused or could not hear.

**Built structures:** The region where settlement arrives, having been near-absent until now. Density itself carries information — the closer to the tower, the more people once lived along the way. Intact interiors are stranger than ruined exteriors: a building whose roof is gone but whose ground floor still holds things arranged the way someone left them does more work than a ruined skyline.

**Light:** Losing sky. First sustained reduction in illumination.

**Frame:** Enclosed, compressed, verticals dominant.

**Tower:** Overhead mass rather than distant object.

---

### Region 4 — The ascent

**Function:** Reversal and payoff. The interior of the thing visible since the first screen.

**Condition:** The only interior in the run. The only light source that is not the sky.

**Frame:** Fully vertical.

**Art economy:** The tower's material language is established from the outside across three regions. The interior is a reveal, not a new visual vocabulary — it costs substantially less than a full region.

---

### Recommended scope for first release

Regions 1, 2, and 4 (arrival, living-and-silent, ascent), with the tower visible throughout. Three regions plus a landmark that resolves across all of them reads as a world with more of itself offscreen — which is the intended feeling. Four or more regions at reduced fidelity reads as thin.

## 5. The tower encounter

The traveller is at the summit, and there is an **optional encounter** with him there, on the belief that only one of them can go through.

Neither knows the mechanism is broken. Two people crossed an emptied world to reach a door that does not open, and one may kill the other for the right to try it first. That is the premise of the game compressed into a single encounter, delivered with no exposition.

### Declining is a real branch

If the player does not fight, **he goes through ahead of them — and it does not work.** The player receives the reveal before climbing further, delivered by the person they spared, and continues anyway because there is nothing else. This is a player-authored ending beat and it is stronger than anything the encounter itself can deliver.

Both branches must be built. The refusal is not a lesser path.

### Open — the source of the scarcity

Where the belief that only one can pass comes from is **unresolved**, and it is the load-bearing beam under the encounter. Nobody has ever returned, so there is no testimony.

- *Two desperate people arriving at an assumption* — plausible, and thin.
- *The tower communicating it* — solves the problem, but the tower now speaks to people, which is a material change to a premise built on indifference.

### Framing constraint

Roguelike players read an optional boss as a reward node. If the player kills him and receives nothing, that reads as a bug rather than a statement, however much the latter is intended. Either attach something mechanical and accept the slight cheapening, or frame the encounter as clearly **not a node** — no reward presentation, no chest, not offered on the map as a routing choice.

## 6. Open questions

**Does the run contain a descent?** Earlier versions of this progression included a descent-then-ascent shape (down to a floor, then up the tower). That shape came from an industrial framing that has since been removed. Under the current premise, the tower is the destination and there is no established reason to go underground first. The alternative — a purely horizontal approach with the tower ascent as the only vertical movement — is cleaner but gives up a strong structural reversal. **Not decided.**

**Where does the second reveal land?** The summit currently carries two beats: the mechanism is broken, and it never needed to work. Stacking both at the top is a lot for one room. The second may be better distributed across the run as environmental evidence — discovered by the player rather than stated to the character. Bible instinct (lore in what you walk past) points this way. **Not decided.**

**Does anything at the top know?** A tower that is simply broken is sad. A tower that has been calling for centuries and *cannot stop* is worse. **Not decided.**

**Final encounter.** Distinct from the traveller encounter above, which is optional. Options under consideration, in rough order of fit:
- *The mechanism* — apparatus cycling an evacuation sequence that no longer completes, running it on a body. No malice, enormous force. Preserves indifference to the last frame.
- *The operator* — someone stayed to run the sequence for everyone else and is still working. The only option that permits speech. Ends on sadness rather than triumph.
- *The queue* — everything that answered, got to the top, and got no further, accumulated over centuries. Pays off the figures crossing the frame in earlier regions.
- *The signal embodied* — the thing producing the call, unable to stop.

A beast guarding the summit is explicitly rejected: it imports a genre convention into a premise that is not about being opposed.

**Whether the traveller encounter and the final encounter are the same event.** If the traveller is the last thing in the game, the ending is human and small. If something else follows him, he becomes the penultimate beat. **Not decided.**

**Does the run start at a threshold?** If the character arrived from somewhere that still has things in it, Region 1 is a boundary crossing rather than a starting point. Cheap to establish, significant implication.

## 7. Not decided here

The following are outside the scope of this document and are not proposed, implied, or settled by it: region names, world-voice text, architectural language, and the game's title.

Everything above concerns function, condition, and constraint. Visual specifics — proportion, geometry, material, ornament, construction logic — are not settled here and are for art direction review.
